## 0. Key findings (TL;DR)

1. **Every Norwegian fishing vessel under 500 GT must have a documented maintenance system.** This is a legal requirement, and it is the most important finding for NAUTECH. *Forskrift om sikkerhetsstyring for mindre lasteskip, passasjerskip og fiskefartøy mv.* (FOR‑2016‑12‑16‑1770) § 9 says the company "skal utvikle, følge opp og dokumentere et vedlikeholdssystem som er tilpasset driftsformen til skipet" and must identify critical equipment. The regulation covers cargo ships under 500 GT, **fishing vessels under 500 GT** and small passenger ships. It has been in force since 1 Jan 2017, with systems required by 1 Jul 2017. Sjøfartsdirektoratet (Sdir) audits it with checklist KS‑1260, which explicitly asks whether bought systems are "ikke eller lite tilpasset driften". Fishing vessels of **500 GT or more** fall under full **ISM** with DOC and SMC certificates (FOR‑2014‑09‑05‑1191). So there is a forced budget for maintenance documentation across the whole professional fleet, but no forced *software* budget. Paper or Excel is legal.
2. **Fishing fleet (register, 31.12.2025):** 4,994 vessels. Of these, 3,909 are under 11 m, 646 are 11–14.99 m, 175 are 15–27.99 m and **264 are 28 m or more (222 distinct legal owners)**. The profitability survey counts the core ocean‑going licence fleet at **153 vessels**: 37 cod trawlers, 22 autoliners, 65 purse seiners, 15 pelagic trawlers and 14 crab vessels. Another 44 vessels are in the SUK group. **Sunnmøre alone has 57 fishing vessels of 28 m or more, held by 55 legal owners.**
3. **The ocean‑going fleet is very well funded.** An average cod trawler had NOK 188 m revenue and NOK 36 m operating profit in 2024. A purse seiner had NOK 121 m and NOK 39 m. A software spend of NOK 300–500k per vessel is 0.2–0.4 % of revenue.
4. **Other segments (Norwegian‑owned, NOR+NIS, 2025, SSB):** 241 car ferries, 701 passenger boats, 160 PSV/AHTS, 147 tugs, 270 general cargo ships, 140 well‑boats, 25 research vessels and 3,213 "work ships", most of which are small aquaculture workboats. Sdir counts the aquaculture fleet at **92 well‑boats, 54 workboats over 15 m and 906 workboats of 8–15 m**, run by about 50 service companies.
5. **Recommended beachhead hypothesis:** *Ocean‑going (and large coastal) fishing vessels of 28 m or more, owned by family and mid‑sized rederier on the Møre/Vestland coast, whose superintendents are drowning in PMS, certificate, crew and quota/ERS paperwork across several disconnected systems.* Bottom‑up TAM is **NOK 23 / 46 / 77 m per year** (low / base / high) on 153 vessels. It rises to **NOK 30 / 59 / 99 m** if the SUK group is included (197 vessels). Aquaculture service vessels of 15 m or more are the strongest second segment, at **NOK 11 / 28 / 55 m**.

**Interpretation.** The popular belief that only ships of 500 GT or more need a maintenance system is wrong for Norway. Since 2017, **every fishing vessel and every small cargo or aquaculture vessel** has needed a documented maintenance system and a critical‑equipment list. The pain is that it is audited but not certified. The "forced budget" is real for about 440 fishing vessels of 15 m or more and about 1,000 aquaculture vessels. The penalty for doing it on paper is an Sdir *pålegg*, not a lost certificate. Urgency is therefore driven more by operational pain and incidents than by fear of losing a certificate.

### 2.2 Class requirements (DNV)

Class does **not** require PMS software. However, the **"Machinery PMS" survey arrangement** lets class‑relevant machinery surveys be credited through an approved, implemented PMS, which saves survey time and off‑hire. Since 1 Jan 2025 the DNV Fleet‑in‑Service rules merge MPMS, MCBM, DPMS and similar schemes into **PMS.A** ([DNV news](https://www.dnv.com/news/2025/new-dnv-fleet-in-service-rules-available-from-14-june-2025/)). Software used for this must be **type‑approved to DNV‑CP‑0206** (Machinery planned maintenance system). Incumbents hold such certificates, for example Kongsberg K‑Fleet ([certificate](https://kongsberg.com/globalassets/kongsberg-maritime/documents/certificates/product/k-fleet-maintenance-system-for-machinery/dnv-gl/tapms000001x.pdf)) and BASSnet ([certificate](https://www.bassnet.no/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/DNV-Type-Approval-Certification-Exp.-12th-May-2027.pdf)). **Implication:** to sell to classed havfiske and OSV vessels that already use PMS.A, NAUTECH will eventually need DNV‑CP‑0206 type approval. That is a *complementary asset* for the matrix.

### 2.3 Cape Town Agreement (fishing vessels ≥ 24 m)

Norway was the **first state to sign, on 15 Jul 2013**, expressing consent to be bound ([Marinelink](https://www.marinelink.com/archive/20130716); [World Fishing](https://www.worldfishing.net/norway-first-to-sign-safety-agreement/138222.article)). The Agreement enters into force globally on **24 Feb 2027** ([DNV](https://www.dnv.com/news/2026/global-regulatory-framework-for-fishing-vessel-safety-in-force-from-24-february-2027/); [West P&I](https://www.westpandi.com/News-and-Resources/News/April-2026/Cape-Town-Agreement/)). Norwegian national rules already largely match Torremolinos standards, so the main new effect is **port‑state control abroad** (UK, EU, Iceland) of vessels of 24 m or more. That means more documentation checks for about 315 Norwegian vessels of 24 m or more (our register tabulation). Use it as a timely sales hook, not as a legal mandate for new software.

### 2.4 Fisheries reporting (ERS/VMS)

ERS‑forskriften (FOR‑2009‑12‑21‑1743) ([Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2009-12-21-1743)):
- **§ 8 (position reporting):** mandatory for vessels of **10 m or more**. Extended to **8–9.99 m from 1 Jan 2026**. Report at least every 10 minutes.
- **§ 10 (electronic catch reporting):** mandatory for vessels of **10 m or more**, and 8–9.99 m from 2026. Vessels **under 11 m** are exempt from DEP/DCA messages.
- ERS is therefore universal and is handled by existing type‑approved ERS clients. NAUTECH's opportunity is **integration** (quota, catch and ERS data feeding finance and crew pay), not a replacement ERS client. Replacing it would need Fiskeridir approval.

### 2.5 Emissions (MRV, ETS, CII)

- **EU MRV:** covers cargo and passenger ships of 5,000 GT or more. From **1 Jan 2025** it also covers **offshore ships and general cargo ships of 400–4,999 GT**. **EU ETS:** covers cargo and passenger ships of 5,000 GT or more (from 2024) and **offshore ships of 5,000 GT or more from 2027**. Inclusion of 400–5,000 GT offshore and general cargo ships from 2028 is under review. Both apply to Norway through EEA Annex XX ([European Commission FAQ](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/transport-decarbonisation/reducing-emissions-shipping-sector/faq-maritime-transport-eu-emissions-trading-system-ets_en)).
- **Fishing vessels are excluded** from EU MRV. Reg. 2015/757 art. 2 excludes "fish‑catching or fish‑processing ships" ([EUR‑Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32015R0757)). **IMO CII** applies to ships of 5,000 GT or more of listed types, and fishing vessels are not among them ([IMO FAQ](https://www.imo.org/en/MediaCentre/HotTopics/Pages/EEXI-CII-FAQ.aspx)).
- **Implication:** NAUTECH's emissions module is a *forced‑budget* feature for **OSVs of 400 GT or more (about 160–200 Norwegian‑owned vessels)** and coastal general cargo. It is only a *nice‑to‑have* for fishing, for example for customer or retailer CO₂ reporting and Enova applications.

### 2.6 Crew and labour

The **MLC 2006** certificate (Maritime Labour Certificate plus DMLC) is required for ships of **500 GT or more on international voyages** ([ILO MLC](https://www.ilo.org/international-labour-standards/maritime-labour-convention-2006)). Fishing vessels are excluded from MLC. Instead, **ILO C188 (Work in Fishing)** applies. Norway implemented it on 16 Nov 2017, with new hours‑of‑rest rules for fishing vessels ([Sdir](https://www.sdir.no/en/news/convention-on-better-working-conditions-in-the-fishing-sector/)). Rest‑hour logging and crew‑certificate tracking are therefore a documented obligation on fishing vessels too.

| F. OSV (reference only) | ~169 (SSB PSV/AHTS + service) | 150k | 300k | 500k | 25 | 51 | 85 |

**Rationale for the price points.** For segment A:
- **High** is the founder‑reported incumbent level of about NOK 500k, assuming NAUTECH replaces the whole stack.
- **Base** is NOK 300k, the bottom of the competitor list range. This implies NAUTECH is priced about 40 % below the incumbent total cost, which is a plausible switching discount.
- **Low** is NOK 150k, assuming NAUTECH replaces only PMS, certificates and crew while ERP and ERS stay.

For segment D, prices are scaled down because vessels are smaller, crews are smaller and there is no quota or ERS module. For B and E, prices follow international small‑vessel SaaS levels.

**Sanity checks**
- The beachhead TAM of NOK 23–99 m per year is **modest in absolute terms but right‑sized** for a two‑person start. Aulet's guidance is a beachhead TAM of about USD 5–100 m. A2 base is about USD 5.6 m, the bottom of that range. **This argues for defining the beachhead as A2 (havfiske + SUK) and treating D as the planned second segment.** Nordic expansion (Iceland, Faroe Islands, UK) is follow‑on TAM (Step 14), not beachhead.
- **The key uncertainty is how much of the NOK 500k is actually addressable by NAUTECH.** Does it include ERP/accounting, ERS, satellite communications or hardware? Ask in every interview: *"Hva betaler dere totalt per fartøy per år for systemer, og hva inngår?"* ("What do you pay in total per vessel per year for systems, and what does that include?")
- **Owner count vs vessel count.** At 222 legal owners for 264 vessels, the average is about 1.2 vessels per legal entity. Many groups sit behind several AS. Before building the sales funnel, map groups via Brønnøysund (*morselskap*, the parent company). The number of reachable *decision makers* is probably 120–180.

## 6. Open questions for interviews (feeds Step 2 / Step 3)

1. Who owns the budget for maintenance and compliance software, the technical superintendent or the CFO/owner? How often do they switch (contract length)?
2. How many separate systems does a havfiske vessel run today, for PMS, certificates, crew, ERS, quota, finance and documents? What does each cost?
3. Has the rederi received Sdir *pålegg* under § 9 of regulation 1770, or ISM non‑conformities, related to maintenance documentation?
4. Is the vessel on DNV PMS.A (formerly Machinery PMS)? Would losing that credit block a switch? This determines whether DNV‑CP‑0206 type approval is a gating item.
5. Does Cape Town port‑state control (from Feb 2027) change anything for vessels fishing in UK or Iceland waters?
6. For aquaculture service companies: do fish‑farm customers demand maintenance and vessel documentation in tenders? Who are their current vendors?
