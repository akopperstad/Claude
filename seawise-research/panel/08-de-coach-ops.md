# 08 – Disciplined Entrepreneurship coach and operations memo

**Panel role:** Disciplined Entrepreneurship coach (Aulet's 24 steps plus Cheek's *Startup Tactics*) and former startup chief of staff/COO.
**Date:** 30 Sep 2026.
**Inputs:** `_case-brief.md`, `10-roadmap.md`, `00-mit24-workbook.md`, `07-intervjuguide.md`, `01-competitors.md` and `02-segmentation-and-thresholds.md`.
**Brief:** you asked for resistance, not agreement. Sources are listed at the end. Where I describe *Startup Tactics* beyond its verified table of contents, I say so.

---

## 0. Bottom line

The lead advisor's roadmap has the right skeleton. It fails in four places:

1. **It rests on a false premise.** It assumes a DMU contact list exists. It doesn't: the 214 rows are company names only.
2. **It spreads discovery across too many segments,** even though the desk research (`02`) has already done most of step 1.
3. **It has no protocol for using an MVP that already exists without turning discovery into pitching.**
4. **It assumes a calendar that two employed marine engineers cannot run.**

Customers are only reachable in business hours. That limits you far more than the "200 hours/week", and nothing in the roadmap plans around it.

**The Tesla analogy cuts against you, not for you.** Musk's *Secret Master Plan* (2 Aug 2006) was:

> "Build sports car. Use that money to build an affordable car. Use that money to build an even more affordable car."

The Roadster was a narrow, premium product for the buyers who needed it most. Tesla started at the top of the ladder with *one* product, and the mass-market Model 3 came about a decade later.

Seawise built its "Model 3" first: a 12-module ERP "for all vessels over a certain size". The Tesla-faithful move is:

- pick a Roadster, for example "PMS + certificates + crew/lott for fishing vessels of 28 m or more on the Møre/Vestland coast";
- sell it to the operators in the most pain, at a price that reflects value;
- let those references pay for segment two.

"World domination in three years" is not Tesla's timeline. The plan had no dates, and it took a decade. A realistic goal for three years is to dominate the beachhead and open segment two.

## 2. Running DE when the MVP already exists

The founders "started at the wrong end of the ladder". That changes which steps are cheap and which are dangerous.

**Steps that get faster:**

| Step | Why it's faster now |
|---|---|
| 7 (high-level spec) | It becomes a cut list, not a design exercise. |
| 21 (test assumptions) | You can run real usage tests. |
| 22 (MVBP) | It is subtraction plus hardening, not construction. |
| 6 (life-cycle use case) | Onboarding and daily use can be walked through, not imagined. |

**Steps that become MORE important:**

| Step | Why it matters more |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | The product quietly argues for "all vessels". The step 1 criteria must be scored with the product hidden. |
| 3 and 5 | The persona must not be Arne or Kristian at 30. |
| 8 | Built features create the illusion of value. Only customer "as-is" numbers count. |
| 10 | Lovable/Supabase is not a core; anyone can rebuild it in weeks. |
| 12 | Engine-room enthusiasm is not a purchase decision (the FølgOpp slide). |
| 16 | 150 tables of scope tempt cost-plus pricing. |
| 20 | Offline use and security are unknowns that already exist *inside the build*. |

**MVP-in-discovery protocol: three meetings, three purposes.**

| Meeting | Purpose | MVP visible? | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Aulet's primary market research: "inquiry, not advocacy". Use `07` exactly. | **No.** Don't open the laptop. Delete the guide's line "show the MVP for 5 minutes at the end" for all meeting-1 interviews. | Pain, spend, tools, triggers, DMU, referral |
| 2. Playback | Play back what you learned: the step 7 brochure and the step 8 hypothesis. Then run a **10-minute task test** (Tactic 9, user testing): "Import your last PreMaster CSV and find your overdue items." | Yes, but only as a *task*, never a tour | Observed behaviour, what they point at, corrections to the persona |
| 3. Commitment | Needs analysis with the DMU (FølgOpp stage 2→3) | Only the MVBP scope | Data access, intro to the economic buyer, LOI or paid design partner |

**Anti-pitch guardrails:**
1. The note-taker records talk share. More than 30% founder talk in meeting 1 counts as a failed interview.
2. Every note must record at least one fact that **contradicts** a current hypothesis. If there isn't one, you weren't listening.
3. Score only commitments (time, intro, data, money), never compliments.
4. A founder never demos to their own former colleagues first. Those are the friendliest and least representative users.
5. The agent tags every interview for "confirmation risk" (section 4).

## 3. Layering *Startup Tactics* and the ÅKP sales slides

**What is verified.** Cheek's book (Wiley, 2024) has 15 tactics in four stages:

1. **Foundations:** Goals (1), Systems (2).
2. **Market Testing:** Market Research (3), Assets (4), Marketing (5), Sales (6).
3. **Product Development:** Product Roadmap (7), Design (8), User Testing (9), Engineering (10).
4. **Resource Acquisition:** Legal (11), Finance (12), Pitch Deck Design (13), Fundraising (14), Hiring (15).
5. A bonus tactic: Public Speaking.

Cheek calls this an "entrepreneurial order of operations". He stresses that "if you build it, they will come" is generally untrue, and that founders should build demand (waiting lists) *before* the product.

I could not verify the contents of individual chapters. Everything below maps tactic *titles* to Seawise's needs, not claims about what Cheek wrote.

**The ordering lesson.** Seawise did Stage 3 (Engineering) before Stages 1–2. The fix is to run Stages 1–2 now, keep Stage 3 at "maintenance only", and bring Stage 4 in lightly.

| Tactic | Seawise, Oct 2026 – Mar 2027 | ÅKP slide alignment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Goals | One 12-week goal (section 5) with three key results. Leading indicators only. | — |
| 2 Systems | CRM, Friday scoreboard, decision log, agent pipeline (section 4) | "Salgets 4 vegger": processes |
| 3 Market Research | Primary market research in three segments, then depth in the beachhead | Stage 1: find and qualify, users *and* decision-makers |
| 4 Assets | A truthful website ("MVP ready — seeking design partners"), a one-page brochure (step 7), a LinkedIn founder profile, schema.org markup | — |
| 5 Marketing | Challenger insight content aimed at the persona, for example "What Sdir's KS-1260 audit asks about your maintenance system" and "CO₂-compensation fuel documentation". Also a design-partner **waiting list**. | CEB: "teach something new", "avoid landmines" |
| 6 Sales | Next-10 pipeline; SODUS notes on every meeting; multi-thread each account (at least 2 DMU roles by week 8); paid design-partner offer | Four-stage process; "the most enthusiastic are rarely the decision-makers"; 53% of loyalty comes from the sales experience |
| 7–8 Roadmap / Design | Only the keep/park/cut decision and the brochure | — |
| 9 User Testing | Meeting-2 task tests | — |
| 10 Engineering | **Maintenance only.** Security one-pager, backups, remove demo claims, keep the demo tenant stable | — |
| 11 Legal | Duty-of-loyalty review; pilot and data-processing agreement (DPA) template; trademark and name-collision check ("Seawise" and "Nautech") | — |
| 12 Finance | Innovation Norway Oppstartstilskudd 1 after Gate 1; simple cash plan | — |
| 13–14 Pitch / Fundraising | Park until paid design partners exist (Q1–Q2 2027) | — |
| 15 Hiring | None. Revisit when founders quit. | — |

**Seller profiles.** Both founders should take the CEB self-assessment in week 1. Engineers tend to default to "problem solver", the weakest profile in complex sales. Rule: in executive meetings the founder with the most **Challenger** traits leads and the other takes notes.

## 4. Operating system: two employed founders plus AI agents

**The capacity reality.** "200 h/week combined" alongside two full-time jobs is not a plan. It is a fatigue risk for people who do safety-critical work at sea. Plan on **25 h/week each (about 50 combined)**. The real limit is **daytime slots with customers**, not hours.

Map each founder's rotation and off-days into a shared calendar in week 1. Book interviews into:
- off-rotation weekdays;
- lunch slots;
- calls from shore;
- Sunnmøre visits on days off.

**Role split:**

| | Arne (CEO) | Kristian (COO) |
|---|---|---|
| Owns | Customer: steps 1–5, 9, 8, 16. Beachhead decision, persona, next-10, pricing tests | Machine: systems, CRM, scoreboard, agents, steps 12–13 mapping, 20–21 assumption tests, security, legal, funding, website |
| Interviews | Leads fishing interviews | Takes notes in fishing interviews; leads the offshore/aquaculture comparison interviews |
| Veto | Anything customer-facing | Any building work over budget |

Each week one founder is the **"customer founder"** and the other the **"ops founder"**, so that the one at sea is never the bottleneck.

**Weekly cadence (all times short, most of it async):**
- **Monday, 20 minutes (asynchronous, in writing):** each founder writes their top 3 for the week and the interviews booked.
- **Wednesday, 15 minutes (call):** unblock only.
- **Sunday, 60 minutes (call): review.**
  1. Scoreboard.
  2. Interview debrief. The agent's synthesis is read beforehand.
  3. Decision log.
  4. Review the building queue (usually "no").
  5. Health check.
- **Monthly:** gate review with the ÅKP advisor.

**Decision log** (`decisions.md`): date, decision, DE step, evidence (interview IDs), options rejected, reversal trigger, owner. Examples: "Beachhead = fishing ≥ 28 m, Møre/Vestland; reverse if fewer than 5 of 15 confirm high pain."

**Scoreboard: Q4 OKR**

*Objective:* prove that one beachhead wants Nautech enough to commit.

| KR | Measure | Target by 27 Dec |
|---|---|---|
| KR1 | Interviews held with people outside both founders' employers | 35 or more |
| KR2 | Of the KR1 interviews, those held in the beachhead | 20 or more |
| KR3 | Of the KR1 interviews, those held with economic buyers | 8 or more |
| KR4 | Next-10 accounts reached through at least 2 DMU roles | 6 of the 10 |
| KR5 | Written LOIs, or paid design-partner agreements | 3 LOIs **or** 1 paid agreement |

*Tracked but not a target:* building hours, which must stay under budget.

**Agents per step.** Agents draft, and humans decide and talk to customers.

| DE step | Agent does | Humans do |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 4, 14 | Pre-fills the segmentation matrix and bottom-up TAM from `02` and the registers; updates the scores after each batch of interviews | Interviews; the final scoring |
| 9 | Builds account briefs: fleet, owner, news, likely DMU | Named contacts, outreach, calls |
| Interviews | Transcribes and synthesises into the note template; tags pains, spend and DMU; flags **confirmation risk** (a founder led the conversation, no contradicting fact recorded) | Asking questions; judgement |
| 3, 5, 8 | Drafts the persona, the "as-is vs possible" model and the brochure | Validate them with customers |
| 11, 16 | Competitive chart; Van Westendorp-style price questions | Put the questions to customers |
| 12–13 | DMU and procurement maps per account | Multi-threading |
| 20–21 | Assumption register; experiment design | Run the experiments |
| Tactic 5 | Drafts insight articles in Norwegian | Founders edit and publish under their own names |

**Guardrails against building instead of selling:**
1. Building budget: **4 h/week combined**, logged. Every change must cite an interview ID or a step 20 assumption.
2. No new modules, and no new Lovable features, until Gate 3.
3. If fewer than 3 interviews are held in a week, next week has **zero** building hours.
4. The Sunday review reads the scoreboard *before* anything about the product.

**Burnout guardrails:**
- Seawise stays under 25 h/week each.
- One full day off per week.
- No Seawise work in the first 24 h after a sea rotation or on night shift.
- Sleep at least 7 h.
- Monthly red/amber/green self-rating. Two reds in a row triggers a scope cut, not more hours.
- The 12-week plan has a light week at Christmas.

## 6. Top 7 recommendations

1. **Commit to a provisional beachhead now** (fishing vessels of 28 m or more, mid-size owners on the Møre/Vestland coast) and confirm it by 15 Nov. Don't partner with an ASA as design partner in 2026.
2. **Rebuild the target list from the registers.** The current sheet is a list of names, not a list of people who make decisions.
3. **Keep the laptop closed in first meetings.** Use the three-meeting protocol and the talk-share guardrail.
4. **Treat customer daytime as the scarce resource.** Plan interviews around rotations and ignore total hours.
5. **Cap building at 4 h/week, with every change traceable to evidence.** The MVP is a research instrument until Gate 3.
6. **Make the Tesla sequencing real.** Define the "Roadster" by cutting down to 2–3 modules at a premium, value-based price for one segment. Write down which segment is the "Model S" and when you would enter it.
7. **Run one operating system.** That means Sunday scoreboard, decision log, monthly ÅKP gate, agents for synthesis, and humans for the conversations.

## Stop doing

- Calling the product "live". Saying it's "for all vessels over a certain size".
- Treating 214 company names as a DMU list.
- Adding modules. Polishing the UI. Building new AI features.
- Demoing to ex-colleagues and counting their enthusiasm as validation.
- Planning on 200 h/week.
- Pitching investors or large partners before you have paid design partners.
- Using confidential employer information, including the NOK 5M/10-vessel spend figure, in any external material.
