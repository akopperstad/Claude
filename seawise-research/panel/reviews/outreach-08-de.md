# Outreach review 08 (DE coach): first-contact LinkedIn message for discovery interviews

**Links to:** M1 (discovery interviews, DE step 1 primary market research) and Tactic 3 (Market Research) in *Startup Tactics*.

## 1. What in the draft pulls the interview toward the product

The DE rule for primary market research is **inquiry, not advocacy**. The Mom Test rule is to ask about **their past behaviour, not your idea**.

| Draft text | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Broren min og jeg bygger Seawise, et system for vedlikehold og drift av fartøy." | This names a **solution and a product category** before a single question. The recipient now sees a vendor and starts evaluating: "we already have PreMaster", "send me a demo", or polite compliments. It also primes the modules we want to hear about. About 40–50% of the message is about us, not 10%. | Replace it with one honest half-sentence about the *field* ("digitalisering av drift om bord"). Leave out the brand and the product. The brand name also waits for the trademark check (M0). |
| "Ingen salgspitch." | A denial signals the opposite. It is also a claim, not something you demonstrate. | Show it instead: ask a real question and offer something back. |
| "forstå hvordan rederiene faktisk jobber med vedlikehold, sertifikater og klasse" | This is fine as a domain. But it reads as *our* scope (our modules) and is generic. It gives the reader nothing specific to react to. | Anchor on a **recent, concrete event** the reader has lived through: the last class visit or inspection. |
| No question in the message | The reader can only answer yes or no to a meeting, so a "no" gives us zero data. | **Embed one open question about the past.** A reply alone is already primary market research, and it qualifies the pain before the call. |
| "Jeg deler gjerne oppsummeringen … etterpå" | **Keep it.** It gives something back (FølgOpp stage 4, follow-up) and builds the relationship. | Shorten it. |
| "20 minutter en dag de neste to ukene" | This is good: a small, time-boxed ask. | Keep it. |

**Two caveats outside the text:**
1. **Segment fit.** The recipients are in wellboat, offshore and short-sea. That is fine for step 1 *segmentation* interviews, and it gives useful contrast. But they are not in the beachhead hypothesis (non-classed fishing vessels, per review 08 E1). Tag these interviews as "comparison segment" so they don't dilute the Gate 1 count of at least 12 of 20 interviews in the beachhead.
2. **Warm-network bias.** Arne's LinkedIn followers are friendly and over-represent people who like him. Weigh their enthusiasm down. Also mix in cold contacts from the register-based prospect database.

## 2. Recommended message (Norwegian, 418 characters)

> Hei [navn]! Arne her, maskinist fra Herøy. Broren min og jeg jobber med digitalisering av drift om bord, men nå er målet bare å lære: hvordan holder rederier faktisk styr på vedlikehold og sertifikater, og hva stjeler mest tid? Hva tok mest tid sist dere forberedte klassebesøk eller tilsyn? Har du 20 min en dag de neste to ukene til å fortelle mer? Du får oppsummeringen av det vi lærer av alle samtalene.

**Rationale.** Credibility comes first ("maskinist fra Herøy"). What we do takes one half-sentence, with no brand or product named. The question about the last class visit or inspection asks about the past, so it's answerable in the reply and yields data even if they decline. The ask is small and has a deadline, and the summary is something we give back.

**Variant for CEOs** (they ask "why", not "how"). Swap the embedded question for:

> "Hva var den siste driftsrisikoen eller kostnaden rundt vedlikehold som havnet på ditt bord?"

**Follow-up if there is no reply** (after 5–7 days, one time only):

> "Ingen stress om timingen passer dårlig. Om du bare har ett minutt: hva er det mest tungvinte med vedlikeholdsrutinene deres i dag?"
