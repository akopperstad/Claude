# Outreach review 02: first LinkedIn DM for discovery interviews

**From:** Panel member 02, enterprise sales
**Channel:** LinkedIn DM to people who already follow Arne (not a connection note)

## 1. Critique of the current draft

| # | Problem | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **About 60% of the message is about us** (who Arne is, his brother, Seawise, what the system does). Only one sentence is curiosity, and it's about *our* goal ("vi vil forstå"), not *their* situation | Breaks the 90/10 rule. The reader has to decide whether to care before being asked anything |
| 2 | **"Et system for vedlikehold og drift av fartøy"** plus **"Ingen salgspitch"** | Saying "no sales pitch" right after describing a product makes it read as a sales pitch. Senior people have seen this line a thousand times |
| 3 | **No concrete question** they could answer in the DM | The only ask is a 20-minute meeting, a big commitment for a cold-ish contact. A specific question gives them an easy first step, and the reply opens the thread |
| 4 | **Same message for the CEO and the technical manager** | The CEO cares about cost, risk and who decides. The technical manager cares about daily friction and audits. One message fits neither |
| 5 | **"En dag de neste to ukene"** is vague | Offering two concrete slots doubles the booking rate in my experience. The reader just picks one |
| 6 | **Doesn't use "they follow me"** | That's the warmest fact available. Acknowledge it |
| 7 | **The summary offer is good. Keep it** | It's a real give-get and lends credibility |

**Principles for the rewrite:**
- Start with them.
- Ask one question they can answer in 30 seconds.
- Ask for the 20 minutes with two concrete slots.
- Mention Seawise once, in the signature.
- Arne writes as a maskinist (a peer), not as a vendor.

## 2. Recommended messages

### B – reder / CEO / daglig leder
> Hei [navn], takk for at du følger meg! Jeg spør rederier som [rederi] om én ting: Hva koster vedlikehold, sertifikater og klasse dere egentlig – i tid på kontoret, i lisenser og når noe glipper? Og hvem hos dere avgjør hvilke systemer dere bruker? Har du 20 min tirsdag eller torsdag neste uke? Du får oppsummeringen fra alle samtalene.
> Mvh Arne, maskinist fra Herøy (Seawise)

### C – teknisk sjef / fleet manager / superintendent
> Hei [navn], takk for at du følger meg! Som maskinist lurer jeg på hvordan dere i [rederi] gjør det: Hvor ligger vedlikeholdsplan, sertifikater og historikk i dag – og hva måtte dere lete etter sist klassen eller revisor var om bord? Har du 20 min tirsdag eller torsdag neste uke? Du får oppsummeringen fra alle intervjuene.
> Mvh Arne, maskinist fra Herøy (Seawise)

**Variants by segment** (swap in one phrase):

| Segment | Replace "klassen eller revisor" with |
|---|---|
| Wellboat | "Mattilsynet, klassen eller kunden" |
| Offshore | "klassen, oljeselskapet eller en revisor (f.eks. OVID)" |
| Short-sea | "klassen eller PSC" |
| Ship management | "hvordan dere rapporterer til flere eiere" |

## 3. Follow-up after 5 days with no reply

Keep the same thread. The follow-up is shorter and makes the ask smaller.

> Hei igjen [navn] – skjønner at det er travelt. Om 20 min er for mye: hva er den ene tingen med vedlikehold eller klasse du helst skulle sluppet å bruke tid på? Ett kort svar her hjelper meg mye.
> Mvh Arne

**Rules:**
- Send one follow-up only. After that, the contact moves to "phone in 3 weeks" or "meet at an event".
- If they give a one-line answer, reply with thanks and one genuine follow-up question. Then ask for the 20 minutes again.

## 4. Operational notes

- Personalise [rederi] and, where possible, add one specific detail: a newbuild, a new vessel, or a post they wrote. That lifts replies more than any wording change.
- Send Tue–Thu, 07:30–09:00 or 15:00–16:30. Technical managers read LinkedIn before and after the office.
- Log every send, reply and booking in the CRM, and A/B test B vs C wording on 20 sends each before changing anything.
