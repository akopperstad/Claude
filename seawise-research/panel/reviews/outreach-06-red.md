# Outreach red team: first-contact LinkedIn message (Arne)

**Reviewer:** Red team
**Date:** 30 Sept 2026

**Draft under review:**
> "Hei [navn]! Arne Kopperstad her, maskinist fra Herøy. Broren min og jeg bygger Seawise, et system for vedlikehold og drift av fartøy. Nå vil vi forstå hvordan rederiene faktisk jobber med vedlikehold, sertifikater og klasse i dag. Ingen salgspitch. Har du 20 minutter en dag de neste to ukene? Jeg deler gjerne oppsummeringen av alle intervjuene med deg etterpå."

## 0. The problem before the wording: who is being messaged

- **The targets have drifted away from the plan.** The target list is wellboat, offshore and short-sea. PLAN.md says the beachhead is Norwegian fishing, with aquaculture service vessels as Stage 2 and classed offshore only after DNV type approval.
- **These interviews help only if the segment question is still open.** They are a legitimate way to test segments in Gate 1, but only if the scoreboard tags them as *segment-comparison* interviews. Otherwise they are 20 hours spent on buyers you can't sell to before 2028.
- **Offshore carries a conflict of interest.** Offshore and subsea contacts overlap Kristian's employer, DeepOcean (its clients, competitors and vessel owners). Get the lawyer's duty-of-loyalty clearance before messaging offshore contacts.
- **Seawise is the brand, so the landing pages must be clean first.** Recipients will look at Arne's profile and search the name. If seawise.no still says "live, first crews are using it", or the search lands on seawise.com (a different vessel-data company), trust is gone before the reply. **Do not send until the website is truthful.**

## 1. Why a busy CEO or technical manager ignores or declines it

1. **It is about you, not them.** The first two sentences are Arne, his brother and the product. Nothing tells the recipient why *they* were picked or what they get.
2. **"Ingen salgspitch" reads as a sales pitch.** It is the phrase every seller uses. Having just named the product and the category ("system for vedlikehold og drift"), it signals "a demo is coming".
3. **The ask is vague and loads the work onto the recipient.** "A day in the next two weeks" makes the recipient do the scheduling work. Two concrete slots are easier to accept.
4. **The wrong person is asked, with no way out.** A CEO does not do maintenance, certificates and class. There is no "or who should I talk to?" option, so the easiest reply is silence. A referral from the CEO is worth *more* than the CEO's own answers: it is a warm introduction into the DMU.
5. **The offer carries little value.** "A summary of all the interviews" is abstract. "An anonymised summary: how 20 shipowners handle X, and what takes the most time before an audit" is a benchmark, and managers like benchmarks.
6. **It looks like a mass message.** It has no personal line. If 50 followers get the identical text, some will compare notes, and it becomes visibly automated.
7. **"Broren min og jeg" makes the company sound small.** It is warm to peers, but it shrinks you in front of a CEO who worries about supplier risk. Keep it for the conversation.

## 2. What could backfire

- **Mentioning the product.** It turns discovery into an evaluation. The answers get polite and future-tense ("that sounds useful"), which is exactly the Mom Test failure.
  - **Don't hide the product either.** If they ask, answer honestly: "we're building software for this, but right now I'm trying to understand the problem". The LinkedIn profile already shows Seawise, so there is nothing deceptive about leaving it out of the message.
- **The employer conflict.** "Maskinist fra Herøy" is fine. Do **not** mention Lerøy Havfisk or DeepOcean, their data or their systems, and do not message their direct competitors until the lawyer has cleared it.
- **The two-week window.** It creates mild pressure and, if the reply comes in week 3, an awkward situation. Offer two concrete slots next week and "or whenever suits you".
- **Promising the summary.** It creates an obligation. Only promise it if you will actually deliver it. It is also your best follow-up reason (FølgOpp stage 4), so deliver it.
- **Sending to your whole follower list at once.** A burned list cannot be re-messaged. Send in batches of 10–15, measure the reply rate, and improve the wording between batches.

## 3. Counter-version (Norwegian, 446 characters)

> Hei [navn]! Arne Kopperstad, maskinist fra Herøy. Jeg kartlegger hvordan rederier i [brønnbåt/offshore/nærskip] holder styr på vedlikehold, sertifikater og klasse – hva som funker, og hva som spiser tid før revisjon. [Én personlig linje, f.eks. «Så at dere tok levering av X.»] Har du 20 min tirsdag eller torsdag neste uke? Eller er det noen i teknisk avdeling jeg heller bør spørre? Du får en anonymisert oppsummering av funnene.

**Why it is built this way:**
- It opens with a problem they own.
- It has no product and no "no pitch" disclaimer.
- The personal line proves this is not a mass message.
- It offers two concrete slots.
- The referral exit turns a "no" from a CEO into a door to the DMU.
- The benchmark is framed as the value they get back.

**Follow-up (after 5–7 days, once only):**
> "Hei igjen – skjønner at det er travelt. Om det passer bedre med 15 min på telefon, eller at jeg snakker med [teknisk sjef], si fra."

**Measure each batch:**
- reply rate
- how many meetings were booked
- how many referrals to technical staff
- the share of recipients who are economic buyers

If under 15% reply after 2 batches, change the channel: phone, a warm intro via Fiskebåt, Kystrederiene or ÅKP, or a visit in person on Sunnmøre.

## Top 3 risks

1. The product mention plus "ingen salgspitch" signals a pitch, which kills replies and biases the answers.
2. Wrong segment and conflict of interest: offshore sits outside the beachhead, overlaps DeepOcean and is gated by type approval.
3. Sending before seawise.no is truthful: a single search destroys trust.
